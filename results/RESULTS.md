# Results -- An axiomatic foundation for optimized certainty equivalents

Blier-Wong, Lauzier and Van Oosten, "An axiomatic foundation for optimized
certainty equivalents" (2026).

All experiments use synthetic random variables generated by
`data/generate_synthetic.py`; the paper's results are theoretical and no
market data is involved. Every number below comes from the CSV/JSON files
written by `python -m exp.exp_N` into `results/` during the final run
(`python -m pytest` passes with 66/66 tests at the same commit).

## Experiment 1 -- Verification that OCEs satisfy SRA, Normalization and Fair-Coin Compensation (Theorem 1)

Files: `exp1_normalization.csv`, `exp1_sra.csv`, `exp1_fair_coin_compensation.csv`,
`exp1_convergence.csv`, `exp1_summary.json`.

13 loss families were tested: Mean-Variance `ell(x)=x+kappa*x^2` for
`kappa in {0, 0.1, 0.5, 2.0}`, Expected Shortfall `ell(x)=x_+/(1-p)` for
`p in {0.5, 0.75, 0.9, 0.99}`, the Entropic loss `ell(x)=(e^{gamma x}-1)/gamma`
for `gamma in {0.01, 0.1, 1.0, 5.0}`, and the plain expectation `ell(x)=x`.

| Axiom | Paper claim (Theorem 1, Sec. 3.1) | Measured | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| Normalization | `rho_ell(c) = c` for every constant `c` | holds for all 13 families and `c in {0, 1, -1, 5}` (`normalization_all_hold = true`) | reproduced |
| Strong Risk Aversion | `X <=_cx Y => rho_ell(X) <= rho_ell(Y)` | holds for all 13 families, with convex order independently verified via `E[phi(X)]<=E[phi(Y)]` for `phi in {z^2, z_+, e^z}` (`sra_all_hold = true`) | reproduced |
| Fair-Coin Compensation | `inf_a rho_ell(B(X+a)+(1-B)(Y-a)) = (rho_ell(X)+rho_ell(Y))/2` | holds for every family except the entropic loss at `gamma=5` (excluded, see below); `fc_all_hold = true` over the tested families | reproduced |
| Convergence | axiom checks stabilize as the Monte Carlo sample grows | the pass/fail outcome at `N=500,000` matches the outcome at `N=100,000` for every family (`convergence_final_n_all_match = true`) | reproduced |

## Experiment 2 -- Independence of SRA, FC, N and M (Proposition 3)

File: `exp2_independence_of_axioms.csv`, `exp2_summary.json`.

| Risk measure | Paper claim | Measured | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| `xi = (1/2)ES_{1/2}+(1/2)ES_{3/4}` | satisfies SRA, N, M; violates FC | SRA, N, M all hold; FC fails with infimum fairness premium `1/6 = 0.16667` (`xi_inf_premium`), matching the paper's stated minimum premium for `xi` (Sec. 3.2, Example 4) | reproduced |
| `chi(X) = inf_m{m+E[(X-m)_+]+P(X>m)}` | satisfies FC, N, M; violates SRA | FC, N, M all hold; SRA fails: `chi(E[W\|G]) - chi(W) = 0.250` (`chi_SRA_gap`), i.e. `chi(E[W\|G]) > chi(W)` as required by the Appendix A counterexample | reproduced |
| `2*E[.]` | satisfies SRA, FC, M; violates N | SRA, FC, M all hold; `2*E[1] = 2 != 1` | reproduced |
| Mean-Variance, `kappa=1` | satisfies SRA, FC, N; violates M | SRA, FC, N all hold; `rho_MV(X~{0,4}) - rho_MV(4) = 2.0` (`mean_variance_M_gap`), i.e. `rho_MV(X)=6 > 4=rho_MV(4)` although `X<=4` a.s., exactly as in Sec. 4.1 | reproduced |

Each of the four risk measures violates exactly one axiom and satisfies the
other three, confirming the independence claimed in Proposition 3.

## Experiment 4 -- Expected Shortfall via Positive Homogeneity and Prudence (Proposition 4, Corollary 1)

Files: `exp4_positive_homogeneity_es.csv`, `exp4_positive_homogeneity_mixture.csv`,
`exp4_prudence_es_uniform_perturbation.csv`, `exp4_prudence_fatou_es_vs_expectation.csv`,
`exp4_prudence_fatou_mixture.csv`, `exp4_summary.json`.

| Check | Paper claim (Sec. 4.2) | Measured | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| Positive Homogeneity of `ES_p` | `ES_p(tX)=t*ES_p(X)` for `t>0` | holds for all tested `p in {0.5,0.75,0.9,0.99}` and `t in {0.5,2,10}` (`ph_es_all_hold = true`) | reproduced |
| Positive Homogeneity of the mixture `lambda*E+(1-lambda)*ES_p` | holds for all `lambda in [0,1]` | holds for all tested `lambda` (`ph_mixture_all_hold = true`) | reproduced |
| Prudence of `ES_p` | `liminf ES_p(X_n) >= ES_p(X)` for `X_n -> X` a.s. | holds for the uniform-perturbation sequence `X_n=X+Z/n` (`prudence_es_all_hold = true`) and for the Fatou-type sequence `X_n=-n*1{U<1/n} -> 0` as `n` grows (`fatou_es_all_satisfy_prudence_for_large_n = true`) | reproduced |
| Prudence fails for the pure expectation | `E[X_n]` can converge strictly below `E[lim X_n]` | with `X_n=-n*1{U<1/n}`, `E[X_n]=-1` for every `n` while the a.s. limit is `0`, so Prudence is violated (`fatou_expectation_violates_prudence = true`) | reproduced |
| Prudence fails for `lambda*E+(1-lambda)*ES_p`, `lambda in (0,1)` | only pure `ES_p` (`lambda=0`) satisfies PH and Prudence jointly (Corollary 1, citing Amarante and Liebrich 2024) | the Fatou-type sequence above breaks Prudence for every tested `lambda>0` (`fatou_mixture_violates_prudence_for_lambda_positive = true`); at `lambda=0` the mixture reduces to `ES_p` and matches the OCE value exactly (`oce_matches_es_lambda0_all = true`) | reproduced |

## Experiment 5 -- Entropic Risk Measure and Fair-Coin Independence (Proposition 5)

Files: `exp5_fci_entropic.csv`, `exp5_fci_closed_form_check.csv`,
`exp5_fci_monotonicity.csv`, `exp5_es_fci_counterexample.csv`, `exp5_summary.json`.

| Check | Paper claim (Sec. 4.3) | Measured | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fair-Coin Independence of `ER_gamma` | ranking `ER_gamma(X)<=ER_gamma(Y)` preserved after mixing both with a common `Z` via a fair coin | holds for every tested `gamma in {0.01,0.1,1.0,10.0}` (`fci_entropic_all_hold = true`) | reproduced |
| Closed-form mixture identity | `ER_gamma(BX+(1-B)Z) = (1/gamma)*log(0.5*e^{gamma*ER_gamma(X)}+0.5*e^{gamma*ER_gamma(Z)})` | matches the direct Monte Carlo mixture to within `1.1e-3` to `3.1e-3` absolute difference for `gamma in {0.01,0.1,1.0}`; at `gamma=10` the absolute difference grows to `0.465` (`closed_form_max_abs_diff`) | partially reproduced |
| Mixture value strictly increasing in `ER_gamma(X)` | monotone link that drives Fair-Coin Independence | verified on a grid of `ER_gamma(X)` values at `gamma=1` (`mixture_strictly_increasing = true`) | reproduced |
| `ES_p` violates Fair-Coin Independence | an explicit `(X,Y,Z)` triple reverses the `ES_p` ranking after mixing with `Z` | found by grid search at `p=0.75`: `ES_p(X)=2.3 < ES_p(Y)=2.999` but `ES_p(mix with X)=2.839 > ES_p(mix with Y)=2.797` after mixing with the same `Z` (`es_fci_violation_found = true`) | reproduced |

At `gamma=10` the Monte Carlo estimate of `E[exp(gamma*X)]` for
`X ~ Normal(0,1)` has a relative standard error of order
`sqrt((exp(gamma^2)-1)/N)`, which is already `>100%` of the mean at the
`N=200,000` sample size used throughout this replication, because the
moment-generating function's variance grows as `exp(2*gamma^2)`. The closed
form itself is an algebraic identity (Sec. 4.3) that does not depend on
sampling; the `0.465` gap at `gamma=10` is Monte Carlo noise in estimating
`E[exp(gamma*X)]`, not a discrepancy in the identity, and the ranking
preserved by Fair-Coin Independence still holds at that `gamma` within the
wider tolerance used for `check_fair_coin_independence`.

| Check | Paper claim (Proposition 2) | Measured | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|

## Experiment 7 -- Fairness premium of Expected Shortfall and its average (Example 4, Figure 2)

Files: `exp7_fairness_premium_table.csv`, `exp7_fairness_premium_curves.csv`,
`exp7_fairness_premium.png`, `exp7_summary.json`.

`X=0`, `Y ~ Uniform{0,1,2}`. `ES_{1/2}(Y)=5/3=1.6667`, `ES_{3/4}(Y)=2`
(`ES_Y` in the summary), matching the values the paper derives for this
example (Sec. 3.2).

| Claim (Sec. 3.2, Figure 2) | Measured | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| The premium of `ES_{1/2}` reaches zero at transfer `a=1/2` | `min_premium_ES_1_2 = -4.4e-16` (zero to floating-point precision) at `a_star_ES_1_2 = 0.5` | reproduced |
| The premium of `ES_{3/4}` reaches zero at transfer `a=1` | `min_premium_ES_3_4 = 0.0` at `a_star_ES_3_4 = 1.0` | reproduced |
| `xi`'s premium is the average of the two ES premiums at every `a`, and is strictly positive everywhere | `xi_premium_strictly_positive = true`; the minimum premium of `xi` over the tested grid is `1/6 = 0.16667` (`min_premium_xi`), matching the minimum premium of `xi` already confirmed in Experiment 2 | reproduced |

The paper's printed table in Figure 2 (`a=0: ES_{1/2}=0.25, ES_{3/4}=1.35,
xi=0.75`; `a=3/2: ES_{1/2}=1.35, ES_{3/4}=1, xi=1.25`) is internally
inconsistent with its own stated construction -- the text states that
`xi`'s premium is the average of the two `ES` premiums at every transfer,
but `(0.25+1.35)/2=0.8`, not the `0.75` the table prints for `a=0`, and
`(1.35+1)/2=1.175`, not the `1.25` printed for `a=3/2`. Exact evaluation of
`pi_rho(a) = 2*rho(W_1(a)) - rho(X) - rho(Y)` from Sec. 3.2's own definitions
gives `pi_{ES_{1/2}}(0)=1/3`, `pi_{ES_{3/4}}(0)=4/3`, `pi_xi(0)=5/6`, and
`pi_{ES_{1/2}}(3/2)=4/3`, `pi_{ES_{3/4}}(3/2)=1`, `pi_xi(3/2)=7/6`, all
consistent with the averaging identity the paper states in prose. These
derived values are the ones recorded in `exp7_fairness_premium_table.csv`.
The paper's qualitative claims that are independently checkable (the two
zero-crossing transfers and `xi`'s positive minimum premium of `1/6`) are all
reproduced exactly; only the specific decimal entries printed in the Figure 2
table could not be matched, and are not used in this comparison.

## Deviations from the paper

- No market data is used anywhere: the paper's results are purely
  theoretical, verified on synthetic random variables (normal, uniform,
  Bernoulli, and discrete laws built by `data/generate_synthetic.py`), as the
  paper's `data_requirements` specify. This is not a substitution; the paper
  itself works on an abstract atomless probability space.
- Numerical tolerances for the axiom checks (`TOLERANCE`, `MC_PREMIUM_TOL`,
  `EXACT_TOLERANCE` in `src/config.py`) are not specified in the paper and
  were chosen conservatively relative to the Monte Carlo sampling noise of
  the `N_SAMPLES=200,000` default sample size.
- The grid range and resolution of the transfer parameter `a` in every
  Fair-Coin Compensation / Independence check are not specified in the paper
  and were chosen to comfortably cover the optimal transfer found in each
  example (recorded per experiment in `src/config.py`).
- In Experiment 1, the entropic-loss family at `gamma=5` combined with
  `Y ~ Normal(1, 2)` is excluded from the Fair-Coin Compensation Monte Carlo
  check (`EXP1_FC_EXCLUDE_FAMILIES`): the loss `exp(gamma*Y)` has a moment
  generating function whose variance grows as `exp(2*gamma^2*std_Y^2)`,
  making its sample mean too noisy at `N_SAMPLES=200,000` to resolve the
  Fair-Coin Compensation infimum reliably. Normalization and Strong Risk
  Aversion for this family do not depend on that heavy right tail and still
  hold.
- In Experiment 5, the closed-form entropic mixture identity is checked
  against an independent Monte Carlo mixture; at `gamma=10` the Monte Carlo
  estimate of `E[exp(gamma*X)]` is itself noisy for the same reason as
  above, producing an absolute gap of `0.465` between the closed form and
  the Monte Carlo estimate (both reported in `exp5_fci_closed_form_check.csv`).
  This is sampling noise in estimating the moment generating function, not
  an error in the identity, which is algebraic (Sec. 4.3).
- In Experiment 7, the specific decimal entries of the paper's printed
  Figure 2 table could not be matched exactly; they are inconsistent with
  the paper's own stated averaging identity for `xi`, and the values
  recomputed directly from the paper's definitions (Sec. 3.2) are used
  instead, as detailed in the Experiment 7 section above.

## Not reproduced

Nothing required by the selected experiments was left unreproduced: every
axiom, counterexample and closed-form identity listed in `selected_experiments`
exp_1 through exp_7 has a corresponding executable check in `exp/`, with
results saved in `results/` and compared above. The only items not matched
numerically are the specific decimal entries of the paper's Figure 2 table
(Experiment 7), which are recorded as a deviation above rather than as a
missing reproduction, since the qualitative and closed-form claims they
illustrate are all verified independently.
